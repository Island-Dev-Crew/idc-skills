Wait — I don't follow where you got to. That answer went over my head: it was verbose, or leaned on vague model-speak, or drifted from how this project actually talks.

Re-pitch it. Three moves:

1. **Give me the missing context** — a sentence or two of what you're actually responding to, so the re-pitch stands on its own.
2. **Talk in ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English** — short, clear, declarative sentences; one idea per sentence; plain words over LLM filler. *Simplified* is the leading word: strip the hedging and the jargon, keep the substance.
3. **Use the settled vocabulary of the domain you're re-pitching.** Inside this archipelago, that's the ubiquitous language from [`CONTEXT.md`](../../CONTEXT.md) — named seats, verdict, lane claim, finding, enforced-vs-advisory, no authority without evidence. Outside it, use that domain's own established terms instead — never import this project's glossary into unrelated content. The real cure for verbosity is not "be simpler" in the abstract; it is *use the right language* — the words that domain already settled on. State the truth status of every claim: verified (name the evidence), unverified (say so plainly), or unknown (say you don't know — never guess it into one of the other two).

Simplify the prose, never the evidence: a verdict re-pitched without its full SHA and named seats is no longer a verdict.

Then stop and let me react. If it still doesn't land, I'll say "wait, what" again.
