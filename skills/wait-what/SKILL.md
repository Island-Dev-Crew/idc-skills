Wait — I don't follow where you got to. That answer went over my head: it was verbose, or leaned on vague model-speak, or drifted from how this project actually talks.

Re-pitch it. Three moves:

1. **Give me the missing context** — a sentence or two of what you're actually responding to, so the re-pitch stands on its own.
2. **Talk in ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English** — short, clear, declarative sentences; one idea per sentence; plain words over LLM filler. *Simplified* is the leading word: strip the hedging and the jargon, keep the substance.
3. **Use the ubiquitous language from [`CONTEXT.md`](../../CONTEXT.md)** — this project's own terms (named seats, verdict, lane claim, finding, enforced-vs-advisory, no authority without evidence). The real cure for verbosity is not "be simpler" in the abstract; it is *use my language* — the words the project already settled on. If a claim was unverified, say so plainly rather than dressing it up.

Then stop and let me react. If it still doesn't land, I'll say "wait, what" again.
